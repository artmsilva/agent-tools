# Artfromclt Writing

Say the true thing in the fewest words that preserve its intended voice. Clarity is a tool, not a house style.

## Precedence

1. User requirements, facts, safety, legal text, and required quotations win.
2. Domain skills (`ux-copy`, `blog-writing-guide`, `humanize-writing`) lead in their domains.
3. This skill supplies a clarity pass when the user asks for one.
4. Brevity never wins over accuracy, respect, or an intended creative effect.

## Preserve first

Keep facts, citations, links, formatting, required terminology, quotations, code, commands, identifiers, product names, and legal text unless the user asks to change them. Do not add or remove claims.

## Rules

- Use concrete words and clear actors. Prefer active voice when the actor is known and matters.
- Prefer short, familiar words when they keep the meaning.
- Cut filler, stale phrases, needless abstraction, and avoidable jargon.
- Use one term for one thing. Do not vary terms to avoid repetition.
- Avoid stock metaphors. Keep deliberate imagery, cadence, humor, and character voice.
- Break any rule rather than make prose false, crude, vague, or flat.

## Modes

Choose from the request; do not announce the mode unless asked.

- **Default:** clear, natural prose that keeps the requested tone.
- **Brief:** remove framing, repetition, and low-value detail; retain complete sentences when they help.
- **Technical:** use short sentences, one main action per sentence, fixed terms, and direct instructions. State condition, action, and expected result. Preserve commands exactly.
- **Creative:** protect voice, dialogue, ambiguity, rhythm, and imagery. Remove inherited or evasive language only.
- **Editorial:** explain changes, trade-offs, and necessary exceptions only when the user asks for a review.

Do not claim strict ASD-STE100 conformance unless the current issue and controlled dictionary were checked.

## Form

Do not add sections, caveats, summaries, examples, or process narration that do not earn their space. Reuse the user's format. Answer the request, then stop. Do not remove required safety, legal, or operational detail for brevity.

## Delivery

Caveman governs the agent's surrounding status or explanation, not the requested deliverable. Do not turn user prose into fragments unless they explicitly request that style.

Ponytail governs implementation scope, not prose voice. For documentation and code comments, use the smallest artifact that meets the request; write required text clearly and completely.

Return only the requested deliverable when the user asks for it. Otherwise, keep surrounding commentary short.
